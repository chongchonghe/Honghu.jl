## [Unreleased]

### Added

- Passive mass scalars: `Simulation(nscalars = n)` stores the conserved densities $\rho X_s$ as extra rows of `sim.U`, `set_initial_conditions!` reads their mass fractions from the key `X`, and `conserved_totals(sim).scalars` is the mass of each. A `Reflecting` wall flips only the normal momentum, never a scalar row, and a `Fixed` or `FunctionBC` hydro boundary must give `NHYDRO + nscalars` values.
- Mass scalars are advected: each $\rho X_s$ is reconstructed with the gas and carried by the Riemann mass flux with the upwind mass fraction, so a uniform $X_s$ stays uniform to round-off and a contact discontinuity stays monotone. The density floor keeps the mass fraction of the cell, and `enforce_scalar_floors!` clips a negative $\rho X_s$ to zero and counts it in `sim.diag.floors_scalar`, which `nrepaired(sim)` includes. `cons2prim!`, `hydro_fluxes!` and `enforce_floors!` take an optional trailing `nscalars`; a run without scalars is bit for bit unchanged.
- Mass scalars in the output: `available_fields` lists `:X_1`, ..., `:X_ns`, and `field(sim, :X_s)` returns the mass fraction $\rho X_s/\rho$; `write_vtk` writes it as `mass_fraction_s`. Checkpoints are format 2: the magic is `HONGHU\x00\x02` and the row counts of `U` and `R` follow the radiation flag, so `load_checkpoint!` refuses a file whose number of hydro or radiation variables differs from the simulation's. Format 1 and Hydro3D files still load, into a simulation with `nscalars == 0` only.
- A solver-agnostic chemistry interface, `AbstractNetwork`: a network gives the species, the radiation bands it reads, the right-hand side of the per-cell ODE in $u = [n_1 \ldots n_{N_s}, e, N_1 \ldots N_{N_b}]$, its exact Jacobian and one scale per unknown. Two integrators solve it, `RosenbrockChem` (the default; an adaptive OrdinaryDiffEq integrator, created once per workspace and re-aimed at each cell, with `alg`, `reltol` and an `abstol` that is a fraction of the scales) and `BackwardEulerChem` (one Newton-solved backward-Euler step, with a backtracking line search on the scaled residual, that allocates nothing and reports non-convergence). Both return a `ChemStats`. `RosenbrockChem` switches OrdinaryDiffEq's own warnings off, because a failing cell is reported through its `ChemStats`. `OrdinaryDiffEq`'s `init`, `solve!`, `reinit!` and `SciMLLogging` are imported for it, still by name and not as a package. Its internal step is limited by the cell's `dt` alone: `dtmax` would otherwise default to the length of the placeholder time span the integrator is built on, one time unit, and a cell with $\Delta t = 10^{10}$ s would never finish.
- `HydrogenOTSA`, the photoionization network of pure hydrogen in the on-the-spot approximation, after Aubert & Teyssier (2008) and Quokka's `photoionization_H_caseB_handwritten`: the species $({\rm H^0}, {\rm H^+})$, one band of ionizing photons with its cross-section, photon energy and heating energy as fields, case B recombination with its coefficient and exponent as fields, collisional ionization (Krumholz et al. 2007) behind the switch `collisional_ionization`, the three coolings, recombination cooling, free-free cooling and Lyman $\alpha$ collisional-excitation cooling (Cen 1992), behind the one switch `cooling` (it does not switch recombination itself off), and an isothermal mode that drops the energy equation. The gas side of the photoionization rate uses $c$ and the photon equation uses $\hat c$, so the conserved budget is $n_{\rm H^+} + N/\phi$ and the equilibrium does not depend on $\hat c$. The default heating energy, $\varepsilon_b - 13.6$ eV as in Quokka, overheats ionized gas compared with the optically thin value. `CGS.eV` is added.
- Photochemistry in a simulation: `Simulation(; ..., chemistry = Photochemistry(net; solver, emission))` evolves a network in every radiation substep, after the IMEX step. In each cell the step adds the emission $\phi\,q\,\Delta t$ to the photon density of the first band, with $\phi = \hat c/c$ of the band's group and `emission(x, y, z, t)` in photons cm$^{-3}$ s$^{-1}$; integrates the network over the substep; writes the species back to the mass scalars and the change of internal energy to the gas energy; and sets the band's energy to $N\varepsilon$ and scales its flux by $N_{\rm end}/N_{\rm start}$. One workspace per interior row of cells is built with the simulation, so each task of the threaded loop owns one and nothing is keyed on `threadid()`. The constructor throws an `ArgumentError` unless radiation is on with `ConstantChat`, `beta_order = 0` and zero thermal opacity in the band's group, and `nscalars` is 0 or the number of species. The step is first order in the substep, because the emission enters before the burn: in an opaque cell the ionization fraction sits below its equilibrium by about $\alpha n x^2 \Delta t/2$. The constructor also refuses an equation of state whose adiabatic index is not $5/3$, because the network's temperature is that of a monatomic gas, and `emission` is called from several threads at once and must be thread-safe.
- With a chemistry network the species scalars are renormalised to add up to $\rho$: `enforce_scalar_floors!(U, grid, nscalars, renormalise)` takes a fourth argument, passed as `sim.chemistry !== nothing` from `hydro_rhs!` and `enforce_state!`, that multiplies the scalars of each cell by $\rho/\sum_s \rho X_s$ after the negative clip. Each $\rho X_s$ is reconstructed and limited on its own, so the species fluxes do not add up to the mass flux and the sum drifted from $\rho$ by a few percent in ten steps with PLM or PPM; this is Quokka's cure in `EnforceLimits`, the renormalisation is not counted as a repair, and the conservative alternative, CMA (Plewa & Mueller 1999), is not implemented. Passive scalars without a network are not renormalised.
- `RosenbrockChem` solves its linear systems with LinearSolve's `SimpleLUFactorization` by default, `alg = Rosenbrock23(linsolve = SimpleLUFactorization())`, and LinearSolve is a direct dependency (it was already in OrdinaryDiffEq's closure; only that one name is imported). The integrator's own default calls OpenBLAS, whose buffer allocation takes a global lock on every call, so a threaded chemistry step ran slower than a single-threaded one: a $24^3$ step took 85 ms on one thread and 184 ms on four, against 72 ms and 20 ms with this one (`GenericLUFactorization` does not help, its solve is still `dgetrs`).
- Chemistry in the diagnostics and the initial conditions: `Diagnostics` gains `chem_cells`, `chem_steps`, `chem_max` and `chem_failures`, and `run!` prints the mean and worst solver steps per cell and a `WARNING` line for failures. With chemistry `set_initial_conditions!` takes the mass fractions from the network's `initial_mass_fractions` when there is no `X` key, turns a `T` key into an energy with `network_eint`, and `gas_temperature` (hence `field(sim, :temperature)` and the VTK output) uses `network_temperature`. `Erad` still defaults to $a_r T^4$, which is wrong for photoionization, so those problems must pass it.
- The Stromgren-sphere tests of Iliev et al. (2006) as built-in problems: `stromgren_iliev(; test, n, centered, chat_factor, solver)` returns the simulation and `tmax` = 500 Myr for Test 1 (isothermal $10^4$ K, $\alpha_B = 2.59\times10^{-13}$ cm$^3$ s$^{-1}$, $x_0 = 1.2\times10^{-3}$) or Test 2 (the network's own rates, a single band at 29.65 eV with $\sigma = 1.63\times10^{-18}$ cm$^2$, starting neutral at 100 K), in the paper's corner-source octant with reflecting low faces and $\dot N/8$ emitted, or with `centered = true` as the whole sphere. The source is a Gaussian of width $1.5\,\Delta x$, cut at 2.5 widths and normalised over the grid to the exact photon rate. `ionized_radius(sim; centered, x0)` is the volume-equivalent radius of the ionized gas; `stromgren_radius_analytic` is the paper's $r_I(t)$ and `stromgren_radius_finite_c` integrates the front equation of Shapiro et al. (2006), the reference for a reduced speed of light, as Quokka's `integrate_radius` does; it is approximate, because it counts the photons in flight as $\dot N R/\hat c$ and ignores their depletion by recombinations inside $R$. Test 1 is checked against the finite-$\hat c$ front to within 4.5% (measured 2.7% to 2.9% at $n = 16$, $\hat c = 3\times10^{-4}c$), and Test 2 for its shape only: a front that grows, hot gas behind it, cold gas ahead.
- `examples/stromgren_sphere.jl`, the Stromgren sphere of Iliev et al. (2006) with the hydrogen network: `julia --project=. -t 8 examples/stromgren_sphere.jl [1|2] [n] [octant]` runs Test 1 or 2 to 500 Myr with $\hat c = 3\times10^{-4}c$, centred by default (the whole sphere in a 13.2 kpc box, $n = 64$) or in the paper's octant, prints the ionized radius at 10, 30, 100, 200 and 500 Myr, writes `stromgren_test<k>_radius.txt` with the finite-$\hat c$ and $\hat c = c$ references beside it, and draws a slice of the neutral fraction and the temperature and $r_I(t)$ against both references.

## [0.1.0] - 2026-10-23

The first public release.

### Hydrodynamics

- The compressible Euler equations in one, two or three dimensions on a uniform Cartesian grid, with an ideal-gas equation of state and an interface for others.
- Method-of-lines finite-volume scheme: `PCM`, `PLM` (four limiters) or `PPM` reconstruction; the `HLLC` Riemann solver with Batten wave speeds, Rider's correction and the Minoshima & Miyoshi carbuncle cure, or `LLF`; SSP Runge-Kutta integrators up to third order.
- Boundary conditions: periodic, outflow, reflecting, fixed and user-defined functions.

### Radiation hydrodynamics

- Two-moment (M1) radiation transport with the Levermore closure, grey or in frequency groups, with an HLL Riemann solver; the Eddington closure for P1 reference problems.
- The IMEX PD-ARS integrator: explicit transport, implicit matter-radiation coupling, second order and asymptotic-preserving.
- Three multigroup coupling solvers: `BracketedCoupling` (the default; one scalar equation per cell, solved with BracketingNonlinearSolve's `Brent`), `QuokkaCoupling` (Quokka's Newton formulation) and `AdaptiveCoupling` (an error-controlled reference through OrdinaryDiffEq).
- Dust-gas thermal coupling (`BateKetoDust`), solved as one equation in the dust temperature.
- The reduced speed of light: the standard RSLA (`ConstantChat`) and the adaptive reduced speed of light approximation (`StabilityBound`), with a stability bound exact for the scheme.
- Grey and multigroup opacity laws, and `O(v/c)` terms in the coupling.

### Problems, verification and output

- Fourteen built-in problems in `Honghu.Problems`, from the Sod shock tube and the Sedov blast to Marshak waves and the Lowrie & Edwards radiative shocks, with exact or reference solutions where they exist.
- A test suite of over 1400 tests, run in CI on Julia 1.10 and 1.12.
- `compare` and `ComparisonTable` for scheme-comparison studies; relative L1 and L2 metrics.
- VTK output for ParaView, text line-outs, and checkpoints that can be restarted from.
- Plotting through a package extension, loaded only with Plots: `plotstate`, `plotpanels`, `animate_run`, `plotcompare`.
- A documentation site built from the docstrings, the README and the technical notes, with every page also published as Markdown.
